# Problem Statement

## Core problem

AI systems often misinterpret people because they treat isolated outputs as if they were stable beliefs.

Human expression is noisy. People:

- exaggerate
- joke
- vent
- contradict themselves
- shift tone by topic
- speak differently in different communities

Without persistent context, AI systems can overfit to isolated phrases and produce bad judgments, poor personalization, or incorrect summaries.

## User pain

A person should not have to re-explain themselves every time a system reads something they wrote.

They need a durable layer that provides:

- context
- nuance
- self-correction
- topic-specific interpretation rules

## Target users

### Early adopters

- heavy AI users
- founders and operators
- researchers and writers
- people with strong written digital footprints
- people who care about self-representation and privacy

### Later segments

- enterprise users working with AI copilots
- creators and public voices
- power users of personal assistant tools
- developers building agentic products

## Jobs to be done

1. Help my AI assistant understand my statements more accurately.
2. Separate my momentary reactions from my stable views.
3. Let me define how I want to be interpreted in different domains.
4. Make my context reusable across tools.
5. Give me control over what context is visible and to whom.

## Example failure modes today

- A joke is treated as a serious belief.
- Sports tribalism is treated as genuine hostility.
- A temporary frustration is stored as a permanent preference.
- A narrow data point becomes a broad psychological inference.
- A recommendation engine amplifies noise instead of signal.

## Why now

As AI agents become more proactive, the cost of misinterpretation increases.

Misunderstood context will not only affect recommendations. It will affect decisions, messaging, automation, and digital representation.
