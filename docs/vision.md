# Vision

## One-line vision

Give every person a structured, editable, portable context layer so AI agents can understand not only what they said, but what they meant.

## Background

Today, digital systems mostly infer identity from observable behavior:

- likes
- clicks
- follows
- searches
- purchases
- short posts

That produces a shallow and often distorted representation of a person.

In a world of AI agents, that is not enough.

Agents will increasingly:

- recommend on our behalf
- summarize our beliefs
- respond in our voice
- prioritize information for us
- negotiate for us
- filter content for us

If they only rely on raw outputs or behavior traces, they will frequently misread us.

## New thesis

What AI agents need is not only memory.

They need **interpretable personal context**.

That means understanding:

- which topics matter deeply to a person
- which expressions are literal vs emotional vs ironic
- which preferences are stable vs weak vs situational
- which statements are core beliefs vs momentary reactions
- what tone and language patterns are normal for that person

## The gap

There is no standard personal layer that says:

- this is how I think
- this is how I express myself
- this is what I mean when I talk about certain topics
- this is what should and should not be inferred from my words

## Product category hypothesis

A new category may emerge: a personal interpretation layer for AI.

This project explores that category.

## Guiding principles

1. User sovereignty first  
   The person should be able to inspect, edit, and override their own context model.

2. Transparent interpretation  
   The system should explain why it interpreted a statement in a certain way.

3. Topic-based nuance  
   A person may speak very differently about football, politics, work, money, and family.

4. Distinguish identity from expression  
   Not every phrase is a deep belief.

5. Portable context  
   In the long run, the context layer should travel across agents and applications.

6. Permissioned access  
   Different agents should have different levels of visibility.

## Long-term possibility

This could evolve from a single product into:

- a profile standard
- an API for agent interpretation
- a consent and permissions layer
- a protocol for personal AI alignment
